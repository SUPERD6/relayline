## 3. Power analysis and sample sizes

The unit is the task, with one run per task per arm. A1/A2 tasks are written independently. **B and C tasks are
clustered by repository** (B: at least 10 repositories at 6 per repository; C: at least 6 at 4): tasks from one
repository can share difficulty, so the tests below assume more independence than there is. That is why B and C also
require the repository-cluster bootstrap (section 4), and why the power figures for B and C are optimistic: with an
intra-repository correlation of 0.2 and 6 tasks per repository the effective sample is about half. Calculations:
exact enumeration for McNemar; 4 000–20 000 simulations for the others (standard library; the script is in the PR
description; two independent reviews recomputed the numbers).

| Group | Test | Assumed effect | Level | Power at the chosen n | Chosen n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1, A2 (and A1x, A2x) | exact McNemar, two-sided | p(on right, off wrong) = 0.40, reverse 0.05 (about half of round 1's 16/21 codex; there is no round-1 number for Claude Haiku, so this is an assumption) | 0.025 (Holm's stricter step) | 0.94 | **48** |
| same | same | smaller effect: 0.30 / 0.05 | 0.025 | 0.75 | 48 |
| B | Tango lower bound > −0.15 | true difference 0, discordant 10% / 20% / 30% of tasks | one-sided 0.05 | 0.95 / 0.80 / 0.67 | **60** |
| B | same | true difference −0.05, same discordance | one-sided 0.05 | 0.70 / 0.51 / 0.40 | 60 |
| B | GMR upper bound ≤ 0.8 | true GMR 0.60, SD of log ratio 0.5 / 0.8 | one-sided 0.05 | ≥ 0.99 / 0.87 | 60 |

## 4. Statistics

- **Pairing**: by task in every group.
- **Primary tests**: A1, A2, A1x, A2x: exact McNemar on valid pairs (two-sided, with b > c required). B, C: Tango
  score interval for the paired difference in correctness, one-sided 95% lower bound; paired t on per-task log cost
  ratios, one-sided 95% upper bound; total-cost ratio and median-wall ratio (point rules); each in the valid-pair and
  all-rows analyses. A cost of exactly 0 is replaced by $0.0001 before taking logs, and the number of such cases is
  reported.
- **Repository-cluster bootstrap (B, C)**: resample repositories with replacement (all tasks of a drawn repository
  together; all authored tasks form one cluster, `authored`, since they share an author), 10 000 resamples, seed
  20261116; the one-sided 95% percentile bounds of the correctness difference and of
  the per-task geometric-mean cost ratio, on valid pairs (#347).
- **Multiplicity**: A1 and A2 form one family, Holm at familywise α = 0.05; A1x and A2x form a second family (the
  round-1 Codex claims), Holm at 0.05. Both families and all their runs are fixed here before any run, and they answer
  different claims (same-vendor / Codex sub-agent); neither family's result changes the other's verdict. **If one
  group of a family is Unknown, its p-value counts as 1** (the other group is then judged at 0.025), and no verdict of
  a family is written until both its summaries exist. B and C are each one intersection–union test (all parts must
  pass, so no adjustment is needed within them). B and A answer different claims about different settings and are not
  pooled; C is diagnostic. Secondary metrics are descriptive, with unadjusted 95% intervals labelled as such; none of
  them can turn a primary Fail or Unknown into a Pass.
- **Secondary metrics** (reported for every group where they apply; no gate):
  - **Lead-vendor spend** (here: Claude's own spend, since every lead is Claude): per arm, the spend of every call to
    the lead's vendor in the unit (lead, same-vendor sub-agents, same-vendor reviewers), summed by vendor from the
    ledgers (#347), and the per-task geometric-mean ratio with its 95% interval. It is the cost to the user's quota
    with the lead's vendor. In B both arms are almost all one vendor, so it is close to total spend; in C it separates
    the lead from the Codex sub-agent. Total spend stays the primary cost.
  - Correctness rates with numerators and denominators; cost per success; cumulative and median wall time; cap hits
    per arm (B, C); escalation rate and first-rung vs escalation spend (B); share of checks the sensitivity receipt
    marked as already passing before the change (B); Codex reviewer spend (B); hubmem/hubstart (A2, A2x); neutral
    harm check (A1, A1x); strata by task source and by repository (B, C); the number of repositories and the largest
    repository share (B, C); env_clean subset.
- Zeros: no finite cost per success when an arm has 0 successes.

## 5. Interruption and reruns

As in round 1, plus the invalid-row rule in section 0:

- The scripts cannot resume. A group that is stopped keeps every completed row, its spend counts toward the total,
  and it is **Unknown**; it is never resumed. **A group that finished (Pass or Fail) is never rerun.**
- **Who may stop a group:** only the script's own stop rules (budget before the last unit, more than 3 unknown-cost or
  no-dispatch-evidence rows, its environment check) or the operator for a listed cause: another evaluation or pytest
  running on the machine, a reboot or crash of the machine, a CLI version change. No other manual stop. Every stop is
  written into the change log with its cause and the number of completed rows, and the stopped attempt's rows are
  listed in full in the report.
- If the cause is an infrastructure fault, it is fixed in its own PR that changes **evaluation scripts only**. A fix
  that needs a change to ccx itself or to an arm's prompt changes what is tested; the group then is not rerun unless
  the user approves it first, and the change log records the approval and the change. The group is rerun **from the start** on a new pin with a new tag / output directory,
  recorded in the change log (cause, fix PR, new pin) before the rerun. No threshold, sample or task changes on a
  rerun. The files of the stopped attempt are kept.
- If the cause is the machine, rerun from the start without a code change, same rule.
- At most two reruns per group; after that the group is Unknown and the lead stops and asks the user.
